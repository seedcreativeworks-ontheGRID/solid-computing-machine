Cutting onboarding drop-off 42% for a Series A neobank

Northbridge was losing 58% of applicants before they finished identity verification. I reframed the problem from "redesign the KYC screens" to "sequence trust correctly" — and shipped it before their next board meeting.

Client
Northbridge (neobank, Series A)
Role
Lead Product Designer
Timeline
10 weeks
Team
1 PM · 2 engineers · me
Overview The problem Constraints Process Key decisions Outcomes Reflection

Overview

A funnel problem that was actually a sequencing problem.

Northbridge, a Series A neobank targeting freelancers and gig workers, had a strong product and a leaky front door. Applicants downloaded the app, started onboarding, and vanished — most of them before they'd verified their identity. With a board review and CAC-payback targets six weeks out, the founders needed the funnel fixed, not just polished.

The problem

58% of applicants dropped before finishing KYC.

Session recordings and funnel analytics told a clear story: users hit the identity verification step with no context for why a "banking app" suddenly wanted a photo of their passport. Fourteen screens stood between signup and a funded account, several of them redundant, none of them explaining what came next or why it mattered. Verification itself — the highest-friction step — took an average of 6 minutes 40 seconds and had the steepest single drop.

This wasn't a visual design problem. It was a trust and sequencing problem: users were being asked for their most sensitive information before the product had earned it.

Constraints

Real limits, not hypothetical ones.

Regulatory

KYC/AML steps were legally mandatory and could not be removed or skipped — only reordered, clarified, or made to feel earned.

Technical

The identity verification vendor's SDK was fixed for this cycle — no time to re-platform, so the redesign had to work within its existing capture flow.

Timeline

Ten weeks to design, build, test, and ship in phased rollout ahead of the board's funnel review.

Team

Two engineers already committed to other roadmap work part-time — the design had to be buildable in scoped, sequential phases.

Process

Diagnose before redesigning.

I spent the first two weeks entirely on diagnosis before proposing a single new screen.

  • Reviewed 40+ session recordings at each of the 14 funnel steps to find exactly where hesitation and abandonment clustered.
  • Ran a competitive teardown of six neobank onboarding flows to identify shared patterns worth keeping and clichés worth avoiding.
  • Mapped every screen against a single question: does this build trust, collect required information, or just add friction?
  • Prototyped a restructured flow and tested it with 12 users across two rounds, refining copy and sequencing between rounds.
  • Shipped behind a feature flag in three phases, comparing each phase against the legacy flow before rolling forward.

Key decisions

Small reorderings, disproportionate impact.

Show value before asking for ID

Moved a preview of the funded account — routing number, projected first deposit, card design — ahead of identity verification, so users understood exactly what they were unlocking before being asked for sensitive documents.

Consolidated 14 screens into 8

Merged redundant confirmation and transition screens, removing steps that existed for internal state-tracking reasons rather than user need.

Real-time validation microcopy

Replaced generic error states with specific, real-time guidance ("photo too blurry — retake in better light") to cut retry cycles on the highest-friction verification step.

Trust signals at the friction point

Added bank-grade security messaging and regulatory context exactly where users hesitated most, rather than buried in a generic privacy policy link.

Outcomes

Measured against the metrics that mattered to the board.

−42%
Onboarding drop-off, signup to funded account
+19%
Completed-to-funded conversion rate
2m50s
Verification time, down from 6m40s

The redesigned flow was cited directly in Northbridge's Series A board deck as a contributor to improved CAC payback — funnel improvements meant fewer paid signups were needed to hit the same funded-account targets.

Reflection

What I'd tell another startup facing this.

Compliance steps get treated as fixed, so teams default to designing around them cosmetically. The real lever is almost always sequencing and framing — when you ask, and what you've already earned by the time you do. That reframe is worth finding before anyone opens Figma.
